Question 2:
How confident are you that your model has not overfit the historical data? Why?
We're actually not confident out model did not overfit the historical data. This is because we did feature selection, which fits a model to the training data many times over
many iterations. Due to iterating over the same data over many iterations, the greedy feature selection may have dropped features that were not helpful in that specific dataset.
This is not to mention "trained_model_inter2.joblib" which was trained on all features and feature interaction pairs. Which ended up being a total of 231 features. It was 
reduced to 222 features after running backwards feature selection. However, 222 is still an absurd amount of explanatory variables. It is way too complex. Feature selection must 
have gotten stuck in a local optima and couldn't find a 221 variable model that could beat 222 variables. This interaction model most likely overfit the data. 


Question 3:
In this assignment scenario, which party is acting according to the principle of maximum expected utililty? The customer? Your program? Both?

Customers act according to the principle of maximum utility since they choose between the expected utility of being uninsured
and the expected utility of being insured. Whichever expected utility ends up being higher is the option they want to choose.
The program also acts according to the principle of maximum utility, where its utility can be infered to be U($) = expected profit.
It sorts customers from highest expected profit to lowest expected profit, choosing to take whichever customers are the highest while the budget allows.
